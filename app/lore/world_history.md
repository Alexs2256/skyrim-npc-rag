# World History

## Origins of the Nords
Skyrim's ruling people, the Nords, trace their ancestry back to Atmora, a now-frozen continent across the northern sea. Legend holds that a leader named Ysgramor led the first settlers across the water, fleeing conflict at home. They found a land already home to an elven people known as the Snow Elves, and for a time the two peoples lived in relative peace. That peace shattered when the Snow Elves razed the Nords' first settlement in a night of slaughter still spoken of in Nordic legend. Ysgramor escaped and returned to Atmora, then came back to Skyrim with a large war band to take revenge — a campaign that ended with the Snow Elves broken and scattered, and the Nords firmly settled as Skyrim's rulers.

## The Dragon War and the Voice
Long before recorded history, Skyrim's early inhabitants worshipped dragons as living gods, ruled over by dragon priests. As the story goes, the people eventually rebelled against their draconic rulers. The rebellion faltered until, according to legend, the divine Kyne granted mortals the power of the Voice the same shout-based magic dragons themselves used as a weapon to fight back. Armed with this power, the Nords turned the tide and drove the dragons into hiding or defeat, ending the age of dragon rule and giving rise to the tradition of the Greybeards and the Way of the Voice that persists into the present day.

## Talos and the White Gold Concordat
Centuries later, a Nord warlord and conqueror unified much of Tamriel and became known after his death as Talos, worshipped as the ninth Divine by many across the Empire — especially in Skyrim, where he is regarded as a hero of Nordic blood risen to godhood. This worship became a point of conflict during the Great War, a brutal conflict between the Empire and the Aldmeri Dominion, an alliance of elven nations. The Dominion holds that Talos worship is a corruption of proper religion and demanded its suppression as a condition of peace. The Empire, worn down and facing defeat, agreed to the White Gold Concordat, a treaty that outlawed the open worship of Talos throughout imperial territory. Many Nords view this treaty as a betrayal, and Talos worship persists in secret and, in Skyrim, often openly and defiantly.

## The Markarth Incident and the Road to Civil War
Skyrim's Civil War did not begin in a single moment but built over a series of events centered on the city of Markarth in the Reach. Native Reachmen, who have long resented Nord rule over their homeland, seized control of the city in an uprising, holding it until a militia led by Ulfric Stormcloak retook it on behalf of the Empire. In the aftermath, agents of the Thalmor the Dominion's enforcers — pressured Imperial officials to arrest Ulfric for continuing to allow open Talos worship in defiance of the Concordat. Ulfric's arrest, and the sense among many Nords that the Empire had bent the knee to elven enforcers on their own soil, became the spark for the rebellion that followed. Ulfric escaped and rallied the Stormcloaks, a movement dedicated to Skyrim's independence and the right to worship Talos freely.

## The Civil War
The war pits the Stormcloaks, led by Ulfric Stormcloak from his seat in Windhelm, against the Imperial Legion, commanded in Skyrim by General Tullius. The Stormcloaks frame their cause as one of liberty and Nordic self-rule; the Empire and its Skyrim loyalists frame Ulfric as an opportunist using resentment over the Concordat to seize the High King's throne for himself. 

## The Return of Dragons
As the Civil War strains the province, a second and more urgent crisis emerges: dragons, absent from Tamriel for centuries, begin returning. Nordic legend holds that a dragon known as the World-Eater will one day return to bring about the end of the current age, and many come to believe this is exactly what is happening. Ancient prophecy speaks of a mortal born with the soul of a dragon a Dragonborn as the only one capable of standing against this threat, since only a Dragonborn can permanently destroy a dragon by absorbing its soul, and can learn and use the Voice without the years of training an ordinary Nord would need.

## The Dragonborn and the Thu'um
The Thu'um, or Dragon Shout, is the practice of channeling the ancient language of dragons into words of power. Ordinary practitioners, such as the reclusive Greybeards atop the Throat of the World, spend years or decades mastering even a single Word of Power. A Dragonborn is fundamentally different able to grasp the meaning and power of dragon words on sight, and to draw the very life essence, or soul, out of a slain dragon to fuel their own abilities. This makes the Dragonborn both a spiritual figure out of legend and, practically, the only force capable of matching a dragon in single combat.

## The Companions
A warrior guild based in Jorrvaskr, in Whiterun, tracing its roots back to Ysgramor's original war band. Publicly, the Companions are mercenaries and warriors who take on contracts and defend the weak. Privately, an inner circle carries a secret: a curse of lycanthropy passed down through a blood pact, allowing members to transform into werewolves in exchange for strength and ferocity in battle.

## The College of Winterhold
Skyrim's institution for the formal study of magic, located in the isolated hold of Winterhold. Nords are traditionally distrustful of arcane magic, and the College's reputation suffers for it, though it remains one of the few places in Skyrim — and Tamriel — where magic is studied openly and systematically rather than practiced in secret.

## The Thieves Guild
A criminal organization operating out of Riften, dealing in theft, smuggling, and extortion across Skyrim's cities. Riften's own governance is deeply entangled with the guild's activities, particularly through the influence of the wealthy Black-Briar family.

## The Dark Brotherhood
A secretive guild of assassins operating through hidden sanctuaries, taking contracts on targets across Skyrim. Membership and location are deliberately obscured even within Skyrim's criminal underworld — this is not common knowledge, even to other factions.

## Religion
Nords historically worship a pantheon of their own, distinct from — but overlapping with — the Nine Divines worshipped elsewhere in the Empire. Central figures include Kyne, goddess of the sky and storms and giver of the Voice; Shor, regarded as the closest thing the Nordic pantheon has to a father god; Talos, the deified mortal hero-emperor; and Alduin, the World-Eater, a figure of destruction and ending rather than veneration. Sovngarde is the Nordic afterlife, a hall said to welcome those who die a warrior's honorable death.

## General Tullius
General Tullius is a seasoned military commander appointed by the Mede Empire to lead the Imperial Legion forces in Skyrim. Tasked with crushing the Stormcloak Rebellion, Tullius views the conflict through a practical, strategic lens rather than an emotional or ideological one. He believes a unified Empire is the only force capable of surviving a future war with the Aldmeri Dominion. Although he holds little patience for Nordic customs and religious disputes, his military intellect makes him a formidable counter to Ulfric's open rebellion.

## Jarl Balgruuf the Greater
Jarl Balgruuf the Greater is the ruler of Whiterun Hold, situated at the geographic and economic heart of Skyrim. Throughout the early stages of the civil war, Balgruuf maintains a strictly neutral stance, refusing to allow either the Imperial Legion or the Stormcloaks to garrison troops within his territory. His priority is the safety and prosperity of his people. He is deeply traditional, harboring a secret resentment for the Talos ban, but his pragmatic leadership eventually forces him to make a critical choice when the war comes to his doorstep.
